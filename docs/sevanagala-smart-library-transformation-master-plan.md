# Sevanagala Smart Library Transformation Master Plan

## 1. Document Status

### 1.1 Purpose

This master plan defines the long-term transformation of Sevanagala Public Library from its current condition into a safe, comfortable, inclusive, digitally enabled, sustainable community knowledge hub.

It expands the existing Smart Library Development Roadmap into a practical transformation programme covering the building, collection, technology, services, governance, accessibility, sustainability, and community learning.

### 1.2 Standards Direction

The target model is aligned with the principles of:

- IFLA-UNESCO Public Library Manifesto 2022;
- IFLA Guidelines for Green Libraries 2026;
- IFLA accessibility guidance for libraries and library services;
- WCAG 2.2 AA for the public website and digital services.

The project will adapt international guidance to Sevanagala's local climate, community needs, available funding, staffing, and institutional approvals rather than treating any external guideline as a rigid building specification.

## 2. Transformation Vision

### 2.1 Vision Statement

Transform Sevanagala Public Library into a modern, inclusive, climate-comfortable, digitally enabled public library and community learning hub where people can read, study, access trusted information, use digital tools, develop practical skills, and participate in lifelong learning.

### 2.2 Target End State

The final target is not simply a library with computers or air conditioning.

The target is an integrated public service with:

- a safe and comfortable building;
- a relevant and actively managed collection;
- accessible spaces and services;
- reliable connectivity and digital access;
- a searchable catalogue and modern library workflows;
- digital learning facilities;
- children's, youth, study, and community spaces;
- transparent development and support tracking;
- trained staff and maintainable systems;
- community learning programmes;
- sustainable building and equipment practices.

## 3. Preliminary Current-State Baseline

### 3.1 Building Condition

Initial observations indicate that:

- the building is old and in poor physical condition;
- indoor heat is a major comfort problem;
- current space and facilities do not yet meet the intended modern smart-library standard;
- a formal building, electrical, ventilation, lighting, accessibility, and safety assessment is still required.

These observations must be converted into a measured facility survey before capital work is approved.

### 3.2 Collection Baseline

A large library register reportedly contains approximately **7,400 book records**.

Initial information indicates:

- approximately **400 books have already been removed or withdrawn** by the library;
- this would leave approximately **7,000 records/items** before a fresh audit, subject to verification;
- a regular reader's preliminary observation is that only around **1,000 books may currently feel readily usable, relevant, or attractive for normal reading/study**;
- many existing books may be physically poor, outdated, low relevance, low demand, or rarely used;
- at the same time, important books that readers actually want or need appear to be missing.

These numbers are **not yet an official collection audit** and must not be published as verified statistics until the library validates them.

### 3.3 Important Collection Principle

A book must not be withdrawn merely because it is old, unattractive, or rarely borrowed.

Collection decisions must consider:

- physical condition;
- accuracy and currency;
- community need;
- curriculum and study relevance;
- language;
- literary or cultural value;
- local-history value;
- rarity or heritage value;
- number of duplicate copies;
- availability of a better replacement;
- librarian judgement and the responsible authority's withdrawal process.

## 4. Target Standard Model

### 4.1 Stage A — Safe and Functional Library

Minimum outcome:

- structurally safe and weather-protected spaces;
- repaired roof, walls, floors, doors, and windows where required;
- safe electrical wiring and sufficient sockets;
- acceptable indoor temperature and air movement;
- good lighting without severe glare;
- clean and usable toilets;
- safe shelving and furniture;
- clear pathways and basic accessibility;
- fire and emergency arrangements;
- secure storage and basic asset control.

### 4.2 Stage B — Modern Public Library

Adds:

- reorganised and renewed collection;
- clear classification and signage;
- comfortable reading and study furniture;
- children's area;
- youth/student study area;
- quiet reading zone;
- staff/service desk;
- reliable Wi-Fi;
- public digital access;
- printer/scanner services;
- flexible learning/community space.

### 4.3 Stage C — Smart and Digital Library

Adds:

- online public portal;
- searchable catalogue;
- digital membership/library workflows where approved;
- barcode-based inventory and circulation before considering more expensive RFID;
- public computer/digital learning zone;
- digital resources and curated learning links;
- internal operational dashboards;
- needs, projects, and verified support transparency;
- secure role-based staff administration;
- backups, audit logs, and documented operating procedures.

### 4.4 Stage D — Community Knowledge and Learning Hub

Adds:

- recurring digital-literacy programmes;
- English and communication learning;
- career and study guidance;
- coding/STEM introductions;
- entrepreneurship and financial-literacy learning;
- media and information literacy;
- local-history and community knowledge preservation;
- inclusive services and assistive technology;
- partnerships with education, technology, cultural, and community organisations;
- measurable service outcomes and continuous improvement.

**Sevanagala's long-term target is Stage D.**

## 5. Workstream One — Building Rescue and Thermal Comfort

### 5.1 Immediate Assessment

Complete a measured facility survey covering:

- roof type, leaks, heat gain, and ceiling condition;
- walls, floors, doors, and windows;
- room sizes and usable floor area;
- window placement and cross-ventilation;
- direct sunlight and glare;
- existing fans and ventilation;
- current indoor temperature at different times of day;
- electrical board, wiring, sockets, and load capacity;
- lighting levels and fixture condition;
- water supply and toilets;
- drainage and rain exposure;
- security;
- accessibility barriers;
- fire and emergency risks.

### 5.2 Cooling Strategy

Do not treat air conditioning as the first or only solution.

Use the sequence:

1. reduce avoidable solar heat gain;
2. improve roof/ceiling thermal performance where practical;
3. improve natural cross-ventilation;
4. improve shading and glare control;
5. use efficient fans and air movement;
6. zone spaces by use and heat load;
7. use energy-efficient air conditioning only where justified;
8. size cooling only after the room, insulation, occupancy, and electrical load are known.

### 5.3 Indoor Environmental Quality

Target:

- comfortable thermal conditions;
- good indoor air quality;
- low glare;
- appropriate natural light;
- efficient artificial lighting;
- manageable noise;
- clean, maintainable finishes;
- no dampness or water intrusion around books and equipment.

## 6. Workstream Two — Collection Rescue and Renewal

### 6.1 Objective

Move from a collection measured mainly by total book count to a collection measured by **quality, relevance, accessibility, condition, and actual community value**.

### 6.2 Full Collection Audit

Audit every remaining item against a structured record containing:

- accession/register number;
- title;
- author;
- subject/classification;
- language;
- publication year/edition where known;
- number of copies;
- physical condition;
- content currency/accuracy;
- reader demand;
- curriculum/study relevance;
- local/cultural/heritage value;
- decision category;
- replacement priority;
- notes.

### 6.3 Audit Decision Categories

Use these categories:

- **Core Active — Keep:** useful, relevant, suitable for active shelves.
- **Keep + Repair:** useful but needs binding/cover/page repair.
- **Special/Heritage — Retain:** local, historical, rare, cultural, or otherwise valuable even if circulation is low.
- **Low-Demand Reserve — Retain/Review:** not currently popular but still valid and potentially useful.
- **Update/Replace:** subject is needed but edition/content is obsolete or physical condition is poor.
- **Review for Withdrawal:** possible removal subject to librarian and authority review.
- **Withdrawn — Approved:** formally withdrawn through the proper library process.

### 6.4 Collection Gap Analysis

Build a separate list of what readers actually need but cannot currently find.

Priority domains should include, according to verified local demand:

- Sinhala, Tamil, and English reading;
- children's books;
- young-adult reading;
- O/L learning;
- A/L learning;
- mathematics;
- sciences;
- ICT and computer science;
- coding;
- AI and data literacy;
- English and communication;
- business, economics, and entrepreneurship;
- financial literacy;
- career and vocational guidance;
- agriculture and environment;
- health-information resources from reliable publishers;
- literature and arts;
- history, geography, civics, culture, and local knowledge;
- general reference and lifelong-learning resources.

### 6.5 Replacement Principle

Do not aim to replace thousands of books blindly.

First identify:

- high-demand gaps;
- essential subjects with obsolete material;
- books that are physically unusable but worth replacing;
- missing children's/youth content;
- missing current study resources;
- missing modern digital/technology knowledge;
- missing materials requested repeatedly by users.

### 6.6 Collection Success Measures

Track:

- percentage of collection audited;
- percentage of active collection in usable condition;
- high-priority gaps filled;
- requests satisfied;
- new-title usage;
- repaired items returned to service;
- formally reviewed/withdrawn items;
- language and age-group coverage;
- user satisfaction.

## 7. Workstream Three — Interior and Space Zoning

### 7.1 Functional Zones

Subject to the actual floor plan, create:

- entrance and help/service desk;
- general collection;
- quiet reading/study zone;
- individual study desks;
- group learning table area;
- children's reading zone;
- youth/student zone;
- digital learning zone;
- printer/scanner point;
- community/training space;
- new arrivals and recommended books display;
- local-history/community collection area where appropriate;
- staff workspace;
- secure equipment/storage area.

### 7.2 Furniture

Furniture should be:

- durable;
- easy to clean;
- ergonomically appropriate;
- movable where flexibility is useful;
- stable and safe;
- suitable for children where required;
- accessible to users with disabilities.

Avoid buying furniture before the room layout and measurements are complete.

## 8. Workstream Four — Accessibility and Inclusion

### 8.1 Physical Access

Assess and improve:

- entrance accessibility;
- ramps or level access where required;
- door widths;
- clear circulation paths;
- reachable shelving;
- accessible seating and desks;
- accessible service desk position;
- readable signage;
- toilet accessibility where feasible;
- safe movement for people with mobility or visual disabilities.

### 8.2 Information Access

Plan for:

- clear high-contrast signage;
- simple navigation;
- large-print options where possible;
- audio/accessible digital resources;
- assistive technology according to actual community need;
- staff awareness and inclusive service practice.

### 8.3 Digital Access

The website and future digital services should target WCAG 2.2 AA.

## 9. Workstream Five — Power, Connectivity, and ICT

### 9.1 Electrical Readiness

Before computer or cooling expansion verify:

- electrical safety;
- available load;
- additional circuits;
- earthing;
- surge protection;
- UPS requirements;
- safe cable routes;
- enough sockets for planned zones.

### 9.2 Network

Provide:

- reliable internet connection;
- secure staff network;
- separate public Wi-Fi where practical;
- appropriate access-point coverage;
- router/firewall controls;
- documented passwords and ownership;
- recurring-cost plan;
- backup connectivity option if justified.

### 9.3 Digital Learning Equipment

Final quantities depend on floor space, electrical capacity, demand, funding, and maintenance capability.

Possible equipment:

- public desktop computers;
- staff computers;
- selected laptops/tablets where useful;
- printer/scanner;
- projector or large display for learning sessions;
- charging points;
- UPS/surge protection;
- network equipment.

Every device must have an asset ID, owner, location, warranty information, maintenance record, and replacement plan.

## 10. Workstream Six — Library Automation and Digital Services

### 10.1 First Automation Layer

Prioritise:

- clean digital catalogue;
- book inventory;
- unique item identifiers;
- barcode workflow;
- searchable OPAC/catalogue;
- new-arrivals listing.

### 10.2 Later Circulation Services

After staff requirements and privacy controls are approved, consider:

- member management;
- issue/return;
- reservations;
- overdue workflow;
- circulation statistics.

### 10.3 RFID

RFID should not be purchased merely to appear "smart."

Consider RFID only if:

- collection data is already clean;
- barcode/inventory workflow is stable;
- scale and staff workload justify it;
- maintenance and replacement costs are sustainable.

Priorities include:

## 19. Current Priority Decision

### 19.1 Priority Order

Based on current preliminary observations, the transformation should begin in this order:

1. **Measure and verify the current condition.**
2. **Fix safety, heat, ventilation, electrical, and basic comfort problems.**
3. **Audit and rescue the collection.**
4. **Acquire high-priority missing books/resources.**
5. **Reorganise space and furniture.**
6. **Establish reliable connectivity and digital-learning access.**
7. **Digitise catalogue and library workflows.**
8. **Expand to smart services and community programmes.**

This order prevents the project from spending heavily on visible technology while the building, collection, and core reader experience remain weak.
