# Collection Renewal and Audit Framework

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Objective

Provide a practical method for reviewing the Sevanagala Public Library collection before large-scale replacement, donation, or withdrawal decisions.

## 2. Starting Point

### 2.1 Preliminary Figures

Current planning information indicates:

- approximately 7,400 records/items in the main register;
- approximately 400 already removed/withdrawn;
- approximately 7,000 potentially remaining before verification;
- a preliminary reader observation that only around 1,000 currently feel readily usable/relevant for normal use.

These figures must be verified by library staff and a physical inventory.

## 3. Core Rule

### 3.1 No Mass Disposal by Guess

Do not dispose of books simply because they are:

- old;
- rarely borrowed;
- visually unattractive;
- outside one user's interests.

Every decision should consider condition, accuracy, demand, cultural value, local relevance, rarity, duplication, and replacement options.

## 4. Audit Record

### 4.1 Fields

For each item record:

- accession/register number;
- title;
- author;
- classification/subject;
- language;
- publication year/edition;
- copy count;
- physical condition;
- content currency;
- demand;
- study/curriculum relevance;
- literary/cultural/local value;
- decision;
- replacement priority;
- notes.

## 5. Condition Scale

### 5.1 Suggested Scale

- **A — Good:** ready for normal use.
- **B — Fair:** usable; minor repair/cleaning may help.
- **C — Poor but Repairable:** useful content; repair needed.
- **D — Unusable:** major damage or loss of usability; replacement/withdrawal review required.

Physical condition does not determine intellectual or heritage value.

## 6. Relevance Scale

### 6.1 Suggested Scale

- **High:** current need, frequent demand, curriculum/reference need, or strong reading value.
- **Medium:** valid and useful but lower current demand.
- **Special:** heritage, local history, rare, cultural, or archival value.
- **Low/Outdated:** little current value or materially outdated content; review required.

## 7. Final Decision Categories

### 7.1 Actions

- Core Active — Keep
- Keep + Repair
- Special/Heritage — Retain
- Low-Demand Reserve — Retain/Review
- Update/Replace
- Review for Withdrawal
- Withdrawn — Approved

## 8. Gap Register

### 8.1 Missing Resources

Keep a separate demand list so the audit does not focus only on removing books.

Capture:

- exact requested title;
- author;
- language;
- subject;
- age/education level;
- number of requests;
- exact title required or equivalent acceptable;
- priority;
- reason;
- estimated copies needed.

## 9. Audit Workflow

### 9.1 Process

1. Verify the master register.
2. Physically match shelf items to records.
3. Record missing/duplicate/unrecorded items.
4. Apply condition and relevance assessment.
5. Flag special/heritage items.
6. Create repair queue.
7. Create update/replace queue.
8. Create withdrawal-review queue.
9. Obtain librarian/authority decision where required.
10. Update the catalogue/register only after the decision.
11. Build donor/acquisition lists from verified gaps.

## 10. Pilot Method

### 10.1 Start Small

Before auditing thousands of books, test the method on one shelf or one subject area.

Use the pilot to confirm:

- fields are practical;
- staff understand categories;
- decisions are consistent;
- time per item is realistic.

Then scale to the full collection.

## 11. Priority Audit Order

### 11.1 Recommended Sequence

Start with areas where outdated information or current demand matters most:

1. school/exam and study resources;
2. science, technology, ICT, computing, health-information, business, and reference;
3. children's and youth collections;
4. high-demand literature and general reading;
5. remaining subjects;
6. local history, rare, special, and heritage material with extra care.

## 12. Outputs

### 12.1 Required Reports

The completed audit should produce:

- verified collection total;
- active usable collection count;
- repair count;
- replacement/update count;
- withdrawal-review count;
- formally withdrawn count;
- books by subject;
- books by language;
- age/curriculum coverage gaps;
- high-priority acquisition list;
- donor-ready category packages.

## 13. Public Reporting

### 13.1 Transparency

Only verified aggregate figures should be published.

Do not publicly label individual books as "bad" or "useless." Use neutral operational language such as:

- repair required;
- update/replacement recommended;
- under review;
- withdrawn through approved process.

## 14. Relationship to the Master Plan

### 14.1 Programme Role

The collection audit is a core workstream of the Sevanagala Smart Library Transformation Master Plan.

The aim is not to maximise the number of books on shelves. The aim is to create a collection that is relevant, diverse, accessible, well-maintained, and genuinely useful to the community.
