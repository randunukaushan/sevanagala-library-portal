# Institutional Approval and Governance

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Objective

Define the approvals, ownership, responsibilities, and governance controls required before the portal is presented as an official public website.

### 1.2 Principle

The technical team may build a prototype before formal approval, but official publication, branding, contact information, institutional claims, and donor-facing authority must be approved by the responsible library or public authority.

## 2. Approval Areas

### 2.1 Website Approval

Confirm in writing:

- permission to create the website;
- permission to publish it as an official library website;
- responsible authority;
- authorised contact person;
- approved public launch date.

### 2.2 Branding Approval

Confirm permission to use:

- official library name;
- local-authority name;
- government or institutional logos;
- emblems;
- photographs of the library;
- official letterhead references.

### 2.3 Content Approval

Confirm who may approve:

- About page;
- opening hours;
- public contact details;
- development needs;
- donor acknowledgements;
- project progress;
- public statistics.

### 2.4 Donor and Partnership Approval

Confirm:

- who may accept donations;
- who may communicate with donors;
- who may sign requests or acknowledgement letters;
- whether donor names/logos may be published;
- whether corporate partners may be recognised publicly.

### 2.5 Financial Approval

No online financial collection should be enabled until the responsible authority confirms:

- approved account;
- receipt process;
- accounting process;
- procurement rules;
- audit requirements;
- refund/correction process.

## 3. Ownership

### 3.1 Domain Ownership

The final domain should be controlled by the responsible institution or an approved institutional account.

### 3.2 GitHub Ownership

During development, the repository may remain in the developer's private account.

Before long-term institutional handover, decide whether to:

- transfer repository ownership;
- add institutional administrators;
- maintain an approved technical-maintainer arrangement.

### 3.3 Hosting Ownership

Production hosting and database access should not permanently depend on one volunteer's personal credentials.

## 4. Governance Roles

### 4.1 Institutional Sponsor

Responsible for:

- authorising the project;
- resolving policy questions;
- approving public launch.

### 4.2 Library Content Owner

Responsible for:

- factual accuracy;
- library information;
- needs verification;
- donor acknowledgements;
- public updates.

### 4.3 Technical Maintainer

Responsible for:

- code;
- deployment;
- security;
- backups;
- incident response.

### 4.4 Data Steward

Responsible for:

- collection data quality;
- donor records;
- personal-data handling;
- retention.

## 5. Approval Register

### 5.1 Required Fields

Maintain a small register containing:

- approval item;
- authority;
- date requested;
- date approved;
- evidence/document reference;
- conditions;
- expiry/review date;
- status.

### 5.2 Example Statuses

- Not Requested
- Requested
- Under Review
- Approved
- Approved with Conditions
- Rejected
- Expired

## 6. Prototype Rules

### 6.1 Allowed

Before official approval:

- UI prototypes;
- sample needs;
- sample donors;
- test projects;
- placeholder library content.

### 6.2 Not Allowed

Before approval:

- claiming official status;
- publishing unapproved logos;
- publishing private staff details;
- collecting money;
- publishing real donor claims without verification.

## 7. Launch Gate

### 7.1 Minimum Approval Checklist

Before public launch confirm:

- official website permission;
- official name;
- approved contact details;
- approved branding;
- content approver;
- donor acknowledgement policy;
- privacy notice;
- domain ownership;
- admin ownership;
- incident contact.

## 8. Change Governance

### 8.1 Policy Changes

Changes affecting:

- donations;
- privacy;
- financial features;
- official branding;
- staff authority;
- public statistics

must be documented and approved before release.

### 8.2 Technical Changes

Routine technical changes may follow the Git workflow if they do not change institutional policy.

## 9. Evidence Storage

### 9.1 Approval Evidence

Store approval evidence in a protected location.

Do not publish internal approval letters automatically.

### 9.2 Repository References

Repository documentation may record that approval exists without embedding sensitive documents.
